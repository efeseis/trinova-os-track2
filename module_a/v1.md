## 1. Overview

This project implements a computational prioritization pipeline to identify existing drugs and investigational compounds that may warrant further study based on biological mechanisms associated with the molecular profile under investigation.

The pipeline integrates consequence-aware genomic evidence, a previously generated BUB1B molecular profile, mechanistic compatibility, and drug-development status information from the Broad Repurposing Hub dataset.

Its primary objective is to produce a structured and diversified set of candidates for further investigation.

**These results are research hypotheses, not evidence that a medication works. They are not clinical recommendations, prescriptions, or treatment guidance.**

---

## 2. Scientific Rationale

The analysis is centered on the molecular and functional context of **BUB1B**, a gene associated with the mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint and chromosome segregation.

The upstream molecular profile provides information about:

- Functional variant consequences.
- A potential loss-of-function hypothesis.
- Mechanistic axes associated with the molecular findings.
- Variant phase information, when supported by available evidence.

The script uses this information to evaluate whether drug-associated mechanisms have a plausible relationship with the biological processes represented in the molecular profile.

The analysis considers several mechanistic families, including:

- MPS1 / TTK / spindle assembly checkpoint.
- Aurora and PLK signaling.
- Microtubule-related mechanisms.
- Chromosome segregation.
- Cell-cycle regulation.
- DNA repair.
- Apoptosis.

These categories are used for computational prioritization. A mechanistic relationship does not establish that a drug can correct the underlying biological alteration.

---

## 3. Pipeline Architecture

The script performs the following operations:

1. Validates the expected input files and directory structure.
2. Loads the upstream BUB1B molecular profile.
3. Loads the consequence-aware ranked drug dataset.
4. Normalizes candidate information and development-status labels.
5. Classifies drug-associated mechanisms.
6. Evaluates mechanistic compatibility with the molecular profile.
7. Calculates a composite prioritization score.
8. Separates the candidate collection into a repurposing shortlist and a mechanistic discovery set.
9. Generates exploratory combination hypotheses.
10. Exports structured results, summary information, and quality-control reports.

The pipeline is designed to preserve the distinction between mechanistic prioritization and clinical evidence.

## 5. Mechanistic Classification

Drug candidates are assigned to mechanistic families using information available in their associated target and mechanism descriptions.

The classification process searches for relevant molecular and biological terms.

The principal categories include:

| Mechanistic family | Biological context |
|---|---|
| MPS1 / TTK / SAC | Mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint |
| Aurora / PLK | Mitotic kinase regulation |
| Microtubule | Microtubule dynamics and function |
| Chromosome segregation | Chromosomal distribution during cell division |
| Cell cycle | Cell-cycle regulation |
| DNA repair | DNA damage response and repair |
| Apoptosis | Programmed cell death |
| None / unclassified | No supported family assignment |

This is a rule-based classification approach. It does not constitute a comprehensive biological annotation system.

Candidates that cannot be confidently assigned to a recognized family are not automatically considered mechanistically relevant.

## 6. Candidate Prioritization

The script calculates a composite score using multiple evidence dimensions.

The scoring formula is:

```text
Final Score =
    (Mechanism Score × 0.55)
  + (Original Axis Bonus × 0.15)
  + (Consequence Support × 1.00)
  + (Axis Compatibility × 0.55)
  + (Phase Score × 0.50)
  + (Data Quality × 0.10)
```

### 6.1 Scoring components

**Mechanism score**

Evaluates the relationship between a candidate's associated mechanism and the biological processes represented in the molecular profile.

**Original axis bonus**

Preserves an additional contribution from the upstream prioritization information.

**Consequence support**

Introduces a consequence-aware weighting based on the mechanistic family.

Examples of the relative support values include:

| Mechanistic family | Consequence support |
|---|---:|
| MPS1 / TTK / SAC | 5.00 |
| Aurora / PLK | 4.50 |
| Chromosome segregation | 4.50 |
| Microtubule | 4.00 |
| Cell cycle | 2.50 |
| Apoptosis | 1.00 |
| DNA repair | 0.75 |

These are algorithmic weighting values, not experimentally measured biological effects.

**Axis compatibility**

Evaluates compatibility between the candidate's mechanistic family and the molecular axes supplied by the upstream profile.

**Phase score**

Incorporates available phase-related information. The script does not establish variant phase when explicit supporting evidence is unavailable.

**Data quality**

Provides a limited contribution based on the available candidate information.

### 6.2 Interpretation

The final score is a computational prioritization metric.

It should not be interpreted as:

- A probability of clinical response.
- A measure of drug efficacy.
- A measure of safety.
- A validated prediction of therapeutic benefit.
- Evidence that a drug corrects BUB1B dysfunction.

A higher score indicates stronger compatibility under the assumptions and weights defined by this pipeline.

## 7. Candidate Selection and Diversification

After scoring, candidates are sorted and deduplicated using drug-name and target information.

The pipeline generates two principal candidate collections.

### 7.1 Repurposing shortlist

The shortlist is designed to prioritize candidates with more advanced development-status labels in the source dataset.

The selection includes candidates classified as:

- `LAUNCHED_DATASET`
- `APPROVAL_LABEL_IN_DATASET`

The selection is limited to a maximum of eight candidates, with no more than two candidates per mechanistic family.

**Regulatory-status limitation:** `LAUNCHED_DATASET` represents a status classification in the source dataset. It does not independently establish FDA approval or approval by another regulatory authority. Dataset labels must be verified against authoritative regulatory sources before describing a compound as an approved medication.

### 7.2 Mechanistic discovery set

The discovery set contains candidates outside the two shortlist status categories.

It is intended to preserve potentially relevant mechanisms for further investigation.

Selection is limited to:

- A maximum of twelve candidates.
- No more than three candidates per mechanistic family.

This diversification helps prevent the output from being dominated by a single mechanism.

The discovery set is exploratory and should not be interpreted as a list of clinically available or approved medications.

---

## 8. Combination Hypotheses

The pipeline also generates exploratory combination hypotheses.

It evaluates candidate pairs from the shortlist and discovery set, retaining pairs that belong to different mechanistic families.

The output is limited to a maximum of twenty combinations.

The combination score is calculated using the average of the individual candidate scores.

These combinations are intended to identify potentially complementary biological mechanisms for future research.

**No pharmacological synergy is assumed or demonstrated.**

The script does not evaluate:

- Drug-drug interactions.
- Combined toxicity.
- Pharmacokinetic compatibility.
- Pharmacodynamic synergy.
- Pediatric safety.
- Clinical feasibility.

Combination hypotheses require independent experimental and pharmacological evaluation.

## 11. Limitations and Interpretation

This pipeline is a computational research tool. Its outputs must be interpreted within the limitations of its data sources and assumptions.

### Molecular interpretation

- A STOP_GAINED consequence can support a loss-of-function hypothesis, but does not independently establish clinical causality.
- Missense variants are treated as secondary evidence and are not automatically considered pathogenic.
- Variant phase is not inferred without explicit supporting evidence.
- Upstream molecular-profile assertions are not equivalent to independent validation by this script.

### Mechanistic interpretation

- Mechanistic family assignment uses rule-based text matching.
- Mechanistic compatibility does not establish therapeutic efficacy.
- A candidate's association with a biological pathway does not demonstrate that it can restore the affected molecular function.
- The scoring weights are computational heuristics and require further validation.

### Drug-status interpretation

- Source-dataset status labels are not equivalent to independent regulatory verification.
- `LAUNCHED_DATASET` must not automatically be interpreted as FDA-approved.
- The pipeline does not establish current market availability, indication-specific approval, or pediatric authorization.

### Combination interpretation

- Combination hypotheses do not establish synergy.
- The pipeline does not model drug interactions, combined toxicity, or clinical compatibility.
- Each proposed combination requires independent investigation.

### Clinical limitations

The pipeline does not establish:

- A diagnosis.
- A treatment indication.
- A safe or effective dose.
- Pediatric safety or efficacy.
- Clinical benefit for the individual represented by the molecular profile.

All candidates require independent biological, pharmacological, regulatory, and clinical evaluation before any therapeutic interpretation.

## 12. Reproducibility and Data Provenance

The results depend on:

- The upstream molecular profile.
- The consequence-aware candidate ranking.
- The source drug dataset.
- The classification rules.
- The scoring weights.
- The selection and diversification limits.

For reproducibility, retain the original input datasets and record their versions and provenance.

Changes to upstream variant interpretation, candidate annotations, dataset status labels, or scoring parameters may change the resulting rankings.

The output files should therefore be interpreted in the context of the specific input versions used to generate them.

---

## 13. Research Scope

This implementation addresses the computational prioritization component of Track 2.

Its purpose is to organize existing drug-related information around biological mechanisms and generate candidates for further investigation.

It does not claim to identify a treatment, establish a clinical recommendation, or demonstrate that any medication is effective for the biological condition under study.

The central distinction is:

**Mechanistic plausibility is a starting point for research, not proof of therapeutic benefit.**
